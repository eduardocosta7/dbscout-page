DBScout Privacy Policy

Effective date: October 13, 2026

This policy explains what data DBScout touches, what stays on your computer, what leaves it, where it goes and why. It covers the DBScout desktop app (Windows, macOS and Linux), the DBScout extension for VS Code and compatible editors (such as Antigravity), and the website dbscout.com.br.

DBScout is made by EESystems, doing business as DBScoutTools ("we", "us"). Contact: support@dbscout.com.br. This policy is written to meet the requirements of U.S. federal and state privacy laws (including the California Consumer Privacy Act as amended by the CPRA, and the California Online Privacy Protection Act), and also of the EU and UK GDPR and Brazil's LGPD.

1. The short version

  • No telemetry. We do not collect usage statistics, analytics, crash dumps with your data, or advertising identifiers.
  • Your databases stay on your computer. File paths, database contents, app preferences, table names, package names and SQL you write are never sent to us.
  • Four product connections, none of them for your data: the license check, the desktop updater, the desktop error report (scrubbed before it leaves) and the downloads you ask for. Besides them, the screen loads its typefaces from Google Fonts, and, if you subscribe, the checkout page of our payment provider opens. Each one is described below.
  • The AI assistant is the one exception, and you start it. When you ask the assistant a question, the AI tool you chose on your computer sends your question and what it reads from the databases and app preferences to wherever that tool sends it — for Claude Code, Codex and Gemini, their vendor, under your own account with that vendor (see section 3.7). We do not receive any of it.

2. Data that stays on your computer

DBScout works locally. To show you a database, it copies the database file (and its -wal, -shm or -journal companions) from the phone, or opens the .db file you chose, into the DBScout data folder on your computer. Stored there, and only there:

  • copies (snapshots) of the databases and app preferences files you open, kept to avoid re-copying them over the cable;
  • the copy of a database taken before the AI assistant's first write, so you can restore it;
  • the database snapshots you save (Pro): full copies of a database, each with the name you gave it and the date it was saved. A snapshot holds the data of that database — possibly personal data of other people (section 4) — and is kept on this computer, in the retratos folder inside the DBScout data folder, until you delete it in DBScout or delete that folder. Snapshots never leave your computer;
  • your settings (theme, language and panel sizes) and when the Pro reminder was last shown or postponed;
  • on macOS, the identifiers of the iPhone apps DBScout has found or that you typed in, so it can find them again;
  • the error reports waiting to be sent, and the anonymous sign-in credential used to send them (desktop only, see section 3.3);
  • the AI assistant tools DBScout installed for you, and adb if you installed it from DBScout;
  • the queries you save and the history of the last 50 queries you ran in the SQL query tab (Pro), one file per app (consultas/<app>.json), readable only by your user account. The SQL text can contain data you typed in it — a name or a code in a WHERE, for example. It is never sent anywhere.

DBScout does not keep your open tabs after you close it. The SQL you write is kept only as saved queries and query history, as described above. The app log you open in a tab (Android) is not stored at all: it is shown on screen and leaves memory when you close the tab.

Your license key is kept in your operating system's secure store (Keychain on macOS, Credential Manager on Windows, the Secret Service on Linux), not in a plain file.

You can delete all of this at any time by uninstalling DBScout and deleting its data folder. Uninstalling alone does not delete the data folder, and so does not delete your database snapshots, your saved queries or your query history: delete snapshots and saved queries and clear the query history in DBScout, or delete the folder. We cannot see, access or delete it for you: it never reaches us.

3. Data that leaves your computer

3.1 License check (only if you entered a license key)

When you activate DBScout Pro, and about every 6 hours afterwards, DBScout sends to our license server:

  • your license key;
  • a one-way hash of your computer's identity (it identifies this computer to the license without revealing its name, serial number or user).

The license server runs on Cloudflare Workers (Cloudflare, Inc.) and asks Polar (Polar Software, Inc., our payment and subscription provider) whether the key is valid. When you activate a key, the server sends the computer hash to Polar, which stores it as that key's activation: this is how one key works on one computer at a time. The answer is a signed token tied to this computer. To prevent abuse of the free trial, the server keeps one record per computer that started a trial: the computer hash and the Polar customer ID. That record is kept for as long as the product exists, because its only purpose is to allow one trial per computer.

If you use the free plan without a key, DBScout does not talk to the license server at all.

3.2 Updates (desktop app)

The desktop app checks for a new version when it opens and every 24 hours. It downloads a small latest.json file and, if you accept, the signed installer, from Cloudflare R2 storage. Nothing about you is sent besides what any download reveals (your IP address and the app version). The extension is updated by your editor's marketplace, under its own terms.

3.3 Error reports (desktop app)

When the desktop app hits a defect (an internal error, a crash, a screen that failed to draw), it records a short error report: a random report ID, the time it happened, the app version, the operating system, which part of the program failed, the error message, the source-code location, a short stack trace and whether the failure was fatal (closed a screen or the app).

Before a report is stored or sent, DBScout scrubs it: absolute file paths, package names, database file names and e-mail addresses are replaced by the placeholders <caminho>, <pacote>, <banco> and <email>. Cell contents, the text of your SQL and the lines of an app's log are never part of a report. Expected situations (an unplugged cable, a non-debuggable app, a typo in your SQL) are not reported.

Reports are sent to Google Firebase (Cloud Firestore), project db-inspector-8f280, with an anonymous sign-in that carries no account, name or e-mail. That sign-in does create a pseudonymous installation ID, which stays the same for the reports sent from this installation (its credential is kept in the DBScout data folder), and, as with any connection, Google sees the IP address that sends them. We use reports only to find and fix defects, keep them for up to 12 months and then delete them. The extension does not send error reports.

3.4 Downloads you ask for

When you click to install a component or an AI assistant, DBScout downloads it from the vendor's official address: adb and the Android USB driver from Google (dl.google.com), the Claude Code CLI from Anthropic, the Codex CLI from OpenAI. Before anything runs, DBScout checks the download against a fixed hash and, on macOS, the vendor's signature. Nothing is uploaded.

3.5 Fonts

The DBScout screen loads its typefaces from Google Fonts (fonts.googleapis.com, fonts.gstatic.com). As with any font request, Google sees your IP address. No other content is loaded from the internet by the screen.

3.6 Payments

Subscriptions are sold and charged by Polar, which acts as the merchant of record. Polar collects your name, e-mail, billing address and payment details to process the payment and send your license key. We see your e-mail and subscription status in the Polar dashboard; we never see your full card number. Polar's privacy policy applies to that data. The checkout page is Polar's: in the desktop app it opens in a separate DBScout window, and in the extension, in your browser.

3.7 The AI assistant (Pro)

The AI chat runs the assistant's own command-line tool on your computer. In the desktop app the chat offers Claude Code (Anthropic), Codex (OpenAI), Gemini (Google) and Ollama, and you may add other tools yourself in the assistentes.toml file; the extension's chat offers Claude Code and Codex. When you ask a question, the tool you chose sends the question and the data it reads through DBScout (table names, columns, rows, app preferences) to wherever that tool sends it:

  • Claude Code, Codex and Gemini send it to their vendor, with your account with that vendor. That data is governed by your agreement with the vendor, not by us. Claude Code and Codex also keep their own session history on your computer (in ~/.claude and ~/.codex), under the vendor's rules.
  • Ollama runs the model on your computer, unless you configured it to use a remote model. As DBScout sets it up, it answers without reading your databases.
  • A tool you added yourself sends the data wherever that tool sends it, under its own terms.

DBScout never sees or stores your vendor credentials.

The assistant reaches your databases through a server that DBScout runs only on your computer (127.0.0.1), protected by a random token that changes every time DBScout opens. It is not reachable from other computers. If you connect other AI tools of your editor to DBScout, the same applies to them: the data they read goes to their vendor, under your account.

Do not ask the assistant about a database you are not allowed to share with that vendor.

3.8 The website

The website dbscout.com.br is hosted on GitHub Pages (GitHub, Inc.). As with any website, GitHub may log the IP address and request details of visitors, under GitHub's privacy statement.

4. Personal data inside your databases

The databases you inspect may contain personal data of other people (for example, the users of the app you are developing). For that data, you (or your employer or client) are the controller: you decide what to open, edit, export or show to an AI assistant. DBScout only processes it on your computer, at your command, and does not send it to us.

If you export data to CSV, Excel or TXT, the exported file is yours to protect. The same goes for the database snapshots you save (section 2): they are copies of that data on your computer, and protecting them and deleting them when you no longer need them is up to you.

5. Third-party tools are your choice

DBScout does not require you to use any third-party tool. adb, Xcode, the AI assistants and your editor are distributed by their owners (Google, Apple, Anthropic, OpenAI, Microsoft and others), with whom we have no partnership or data-sharing agreement. When you choose to use them, they process data under their own privacy policies, not this one, and we are not responsible for them.

6. Legal bases (EU, UK and Brazil)

For users in the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom and Brazil, we process the little data that reaches us on these bases:

  • License check and payments: performance of the contract (your subscription).
  • Trial-abuse record: our legitimate interest in offering one free trial per computer.
  • Error reports: our legitimate interest in fixing defects, limited by the scrubbing above.
  • Downloads and updates: performance of the contract (providing the software you use).

How long we keep it. Error reports are kept for up to 12 months and then deleted. The trial record is kept for as long as the product exists. License and payment data is kept by Polar under its own policy.

8. U.S. state privacy rights (California and other states)

In the last 12 months we have collected, from subscribers only: identifiers (license key, a hash of the computer identity, Polar customer ID, e-mail via Polar) and commercial information (subscription status), for the purposes in section 3. From desktop users we may also receive scrubbed error reports (app version, operating system and technical details), with the IP address and anonymous installation ID of the installation that sends them. We collect no sensitive personal information, no precise geolocation, and no biometric data.

We do not sell or share personal information, as "sell" and "share" are defined by the CCPA/CPRA, and we have not done so in the last 12 months. We do not use it for cross-context behavioral advertising or profiling. We do not knowingly collect personal information of consumers under 16.

If you are a resident of California, Colorado, Connecticut, Virginia, Utah, Texas, Oregon or another state with a consumer privacy law, you may have the right to know, access, correct, delete and port your personal information, to opt out of sale, sharing, targeted advertising and profiling (which we do not do), to appeal our decision, and not to be discriminated against for exercising these rights. Send requests to support@dbscout.com.br; we will verify your request by matching the e-mail or license key we hold and answer within 45 days. An authorized agent may act for you with your signed permission.

Do Not Track and Global Privacy Control: DBScout does not track you across sites or apps, so there is nothing for these signals to turn off.

9. Your rights (EU, UK, Brazil and elsewhere)

Under the GDPR, the UK GDPR, the Brazilian LGPD and similar laws, you can ask us to confirm whether we hold data about you, access it, correct it, delete it, port it, or object to its processing. Write to support@dbscout.com.br. Because almost nothing about you reaches us, the answer is usually short: the license key and computer hash (if you subscribed) and the Polar customer record. Requests about payment data may be forwarded to Polar.

You can also complain to your data protection authority (in Brazil, the ANPD).

10. Security

DBScout keeps no secrets inside the program, stores your license key in the system's secure store, signs its license tokens and updates, checks every download against a fixed hash, and keeps the AI server local and token-protected. No system is perfectly secure; if we learn of an incident affecting your data, we will notify you and the authorities as the law requires.

11. Children

DBScout is a tool for software developers. It is not directed at children, and we do not knowingly collect personal information from children under 13 (as defined by the U.S. Children's Online Privacy Protection Act) or under 16. If you believe a child has given us personal information, write to us and we will delete it.

12. Changes

We will publish any change to this policy at https://dbscout.com.br/legal/privacy/ with a new effective date, and describe significant changes in the release notes. Changes never apply retroactively to data already collected without your consent where the law requires it.
